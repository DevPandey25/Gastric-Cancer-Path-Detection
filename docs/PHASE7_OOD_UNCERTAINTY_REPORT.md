## 1. Executive Summary

Phase 7 implements a feature-space out-of-distribution (OOD) and uncertainty screening framework for gastric histopathology patch classification, based on the foundational work of **Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2018)** and the **Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP)** baseline (**Hendrycks & Gimpel, ICLR 2017**).

Using the winning Phase 4.4 frozen **Phikon Mean Patch representation** ($d=768$, $\ell_2$-normalized), we evaluated two complementary novelty scoring mechanisms:
1. **Class-Conditional Mahalanobis Distance:** Class centroids ($\boldsymbol{\mu}_c$) and a pooled shared covariance matrix ($\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$) fitted strictly on training data ($N=2,177$), regularized using the **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage estimator** ($\rho = 0.0202$) and evaluated via a Cholesky solve.
2. **Maximum Softmax Probability (MSP):** Model uncertainty score defined as $1 - \max_c p(c|\mathbf{x})$ from the Phase 4.4 multinomial logistic classifier.

Both scoring functions were calibrated using **validation in-distribution data only** ($N=466$) at the **95th percentile** to establish frozen rejection thresholds prior to test evaluation.

### Key Empirical Findings

1. **In-Distribution False-Rejection Rate Is Well-Calibrated:**
   - **Mahalanobis Distance:** Rejects **5.35%** (25/467) of clean held-out test tiles at the 95th-percentile validation threshold ($1310.16$), closely tracking the theoretical 5% design target.
   - **MSP Baseline:** Rejects **7.07%** (33/467) of clean held-out test tiles at the 95th-percentile validation threshold ($0.6538$).

2. **Mahalanobis Distance Outperforms MSP on Structural Perturbations in Ranking, but Fixed-Threshold Detection Remains Modest:**
   - On **JPEG compression (Quality 50)**: Mahalanobis achieves **AUROC = 0.7958** (FPR@95% TPR = 0.5139) versus MSP **AUROC = 0.4983** (FPR@95% TPR = 0.9315). However, at the fixed 95th-percentile validation threshold ($\tau_{\text{Mahal}} = 1310.16$), the operational detection rate is modest at **11.13%**, illustrating the gap between overall threshold-free ranking (AUROC) and conservative fixed-operating-point rejection.
   - On **Brightness & Contrast ($\times 1.1$)**: Mahalanobis achieves **AUROC = 0.7900** (FPR@95% TPR = 0.6531) with a detection rate of **26.77%** versus MSP **AUROC = 0.4751** (detection rate: 7.07%).
   - On **Gaussian Blur ($\sigma=1.0$)**: Mahalanobis achieves **AUROC = 0.7711** (FPR@95% TPR = 0.7388) with a detection rate of **13.92%** versus MSP **AUROC = 0.5031** (detection rate: 7.28%).
   - On the **Combined Proxy-OOD Set** (all 1,868 perturbed instances derived from the 467 source images pooled): Mahalanobis achieves **AUROC = 0.7157** (detection rate: 14.29%) versus MSP **AUROC = 0.4941** (detection rate: 6.42%).

3. **Softmax Probabilities Under the Four Tested Corruption Families:**
   Specifically across the four tested synthetic acquisition perturbations (brightness/contrast, blur, JPEG compression, and HED stain variation), MSP yields near-chance AUROCs ($\approx 0.47 - 0.50$). For these corruption families, the linear classifier projects drifted features into uncalibrated exterior regions that still produce high top-1 softmax confidence, showing that softmax confidence cannot be relied upon to detect these specific technical distortions.

4. **Synthetic HED Optical-Density Perturbation Behavior:**
   Under the specific synthetic $+10\%$ optical-density H&E stain perturbation evaluated (scaling H and E channels by $1.1$ in Beer-Lambert optical density space), **both Mahalanobis (AUROC = 0.5061)** and **MSP (AUROC = 0.4997)** exhibit near-chance separation from clean tiles, and the detection rate at threshold is only 5.35% (identical to the clean false-rejection rate). This finding is specific to this mathematical HED perturbation and indicates that this degree of optical-density scaling produces representations that remain within the learned feature covariance envelope; it must not be generalized as a claim of universal stain invariance across arbitrary staining protocols, scanners, or real-world laboratory variations.

## 2. Theoretical Framework & Literature

### Lee et al. (NeurIPS 2018): Unified Mahalanobis Framework
Lee et al. model the feature representations of deep neural networks as a mixture of class-conditional Gaussians $\mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_c, \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$ with tied covariance. For an input embedding $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

$$\text{score}_{\text{Mahal}}(\mathbf{x}) = \min_{c \in \{1,\dots,C\}} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_c)^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}^{-1} (\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_c)$$

Key properties:
- Unlike Euclidean distance, Mahalanobis distance scales deviations by the empirical variance along each principal direction, discounting highly variable directions.
- By taking the minimum over all $C=8$ classes, the score measures the distance to the closest learned tissue distribution.
- **Score Direction:** Higher scores indicate greater distance from the training distribution (more OOD-like). Samples with $\text{score} > \tau_{\text{Mahal}}$ are flagged for rejection.

### Hendrycks & Gimpel (ICLR 2017): Maximum Softmax Probability Baseline
The baseline confidence score uses the posterior probability of the predicted class:

$$\text{score}_{\text{MSP}}(\mathbf{x}) = 1 - \max_{c \in \{1,\dots,C\}} p(c|\mathbf{x})$$

Higher scores indicate lower classifier confidence. While widely used as a baseline, modern neural networks are prone to pathological overconfidence outside the training distribution, making MSP fragile.

## 3. Implementation Details & Data Isolation

### Strict Data Partitioning

| Split | Sample Count | Permitted Usage in Phase 7 | Invariants |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Train** | $N=2,177$ | Compute 8 class means $\boldsymbol{\mu}_c$; estimate shared covariance $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}$ | Zero test/val leakage |
| **Validation** | $N=466$ | Calibrate 95th-percentile rejection thresholds $\tau_{\text{Mahal}}$ and $\tau_{\text{MSP}}$ | Threshold frozen before test scoring |
| **Test (Clean)** | $N=467$ | Evaluate ID false-rejection rate and in-distribution baseline scores | Unseen during fitting/calibration |
| **Test (Perturbed)** | $N=1,868$ | Evaluate proxy-OOD detection metrics (AUROC, FPR@95% TPR, detection rate) | Synthetic proxies from Phase 6 |

### Ledoit-Wolf Shrinkage & Conditioning

In a 768-dimensional embedding space with $N_{\text{train}} = 2,177$, the sample covariance matrix $\mathbf{S}$ has an effective sample-to-dimension ratio of $2,177 / 768 \approx 2.83$. An unregularized empirical covariance would suffer from eigenvalue dispersion and numerical instability during inversion.

We applied the **Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage estimator** (`sklearn.covariance.LedoitWolf`):

$$\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{\text{LW}} = (1 - \rho) \mathbf{S} + \rho \frac{\text{Tr}(\mathbf{S})}{d} \mathbf{I}$$

- **Estimated Shrinkage Coefficient ($\rho$):** $0.020206$
- **Eigenvalue Bounds:** $\lambda_{\min} = 8.6117 \times 10^{-6}$, $\lambda_{\max} = 2.6597 \times 10^{-2}$
- **Condition Number:** $\kappa(\boldsymbol{\Sigma}) = 3.09 \times 10^3$ (well-conditioned; all eigenvalues strictly positive)
- **Numerical Computation:** The lower Cholesky factor $\mathbf{L}$ ($\boldsymbol{\Sigma} = \mathbf{L}\mathbf{L}^\top$) was computed via `scipy.linalg.cholesky`. For each query vector $\boldsymbol{\delta}_c = \mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_c$, the triangular system $\mathbf{L}\mathbf{z} = \boldsymbol{\delta}_c$ was solved via forward substitution (`scipy.linalg.solve_triangular`), yielding the exact Mahalanobis distance $\|\mathbf{z}\|_2^2$ without matrix inversion.

## 5. Comparative Evaluation on Synthetic OOD Proxies

We evaluated how effectively Mahalanobis distance and MSP discriminate between clean test images (In-Distribution, label $=0$, $N=467$) and Phase 6 perturbed test images (Synthetic Proxy-OOD, label $=1$, $N=1,868$ generated from 4 distinct transformations of the same 467 source images).

| Perturbation Family | Method | AUROC | FPR@95% TPR | Detection Rate at $\tau_{0.95}$ | Mean OOD Score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| | MSP Baseline | 0.4983 | 0.9315 | 4.71% | $0.3174 \pm 0.160$ |

| | MSP Baseline | 0.4941 | 0.9486 | 6.42% | $0.3156 \pm 0.166$ |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **AUROC vs. Operating-Point Detection Rate at Conservative Thresholds:**  
> A critical scientific distinction must be maintained between threshold-free discriminative potential (AUROC) and the operational detection rate at a conservative threshold calibrated for low false alarms.  
> - While Mahalanobis distance achieves substantial AUROC values on JPEG ($0.7958$), Brightness/Contrast ($0.7900$), and Blur ($0.7711$), its detection rate at the fixed 95th-percentile validation threshold remains modest ($11.13\%$ to $26.77\%$).  
> - This occurs because a 95th-percentile calibration enforces a very low in-distribution false rejection rate ($~5\%$), setting a high rejection cutoff that captures only the most heavily displaced tail of perturbed samples. Achieving a $95\%$ true positive detection rate on these mild synthetic corruptions would require tolerating high false positive rates (FPR@95% TPR = $0.51 - 0.74$), which would be prohibitive in clinical workflows.  
> - Furthermore, the $1,868$ proxy instances are paired derivatives (4 per image) of the $467$ test sources, not $1,868$ independent tissue cases.

> [!NOTE]
> **Why Does MSP Fail While Mahalanobis Succeeds on Tested Corruptions?**  
> Under global illumination changes, blur, and compression, image features are shifted away from the training manifold. However, the classifier's linear hyperplanes partition the entire 768-d space into uncalibrated regions. Even as a feature vector drifts outward into low-density space, its projection onto the winning class weight vector can remain large, yielding a softmax probability near $0.99$. Mahalanobis distance measures the radial distance back to the nearest cluster centroid, immediately detecting that the vector has left the high-density training region.

## 8. Scientific Boundaries & Clinical Limitations

1. **Research Screening Only, Not a Diagnostic Safety Gate:** Mahalanobis and MSP scores are quantitative research flags. They must not be used to label clinical biopsies as "safe" or "unsafe" for automated diagnosis.
2. **Synthetic Corruptions $\neq$ Unseen Biological Pathologies:** The synthetic perturbations evaluated here simulate technical acquisition noise (blur, compression, exposure). They do not represent rare non-neoplastic diseases, metastatic malignancies, or foreign tissue types.
3. **No Synthetic "Unknown" Class:** We strictly refrained from introducing an artificial 9th "unknown" class, preserving the clean multiclass topology.
4. **Gaussian Approximation Scope:** While the Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage covariance effectively captures second-order statistics, higher-order non-Gaussianities in transformer token spaces are not modeled.
